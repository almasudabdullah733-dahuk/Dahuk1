Awareness & Education

Knowledge is your first line of defence. Explore guides, articles, and explainers designed to help young people in Bangladesh understand, recognize, and respond to cyberbullying.

Why Awareness Matters

Cyberbullying thrives in silence. The more young people understand what it looks like and what to do about it. The harder it becomes for harassment to take hold.

These resources are written for students aged 13–25, but they're useful for anyone who wants to better understand the digital world young people navigate every day.

Types of Cyberbullying

Online harassment takes many forms. Recognizing each type is the first step toward stopping it.

Harassment

Most Common

Repeatedly sending offensive, rude, or insulting messages to someone — through social media, messaging apps, or gaming platforms.

Cyberstalking

Serious

Persistently monitoring, following, or contacting someone online without their consent, often with the intent to intimidate or control.

Doxing

Dangerous

Publicly sharing someone's private information — home address, phone number, school — without their permission, putting them at risk.

Impersonation

Deceptive

Creating fake accounts or pretending to be someone else online to damage their reputation, spread false information, or deceive others.

Non-Consensual Sharing

Illegal

Sharing private or intimate images of someone without their consent — also known as 'revenge porn' — which is illegal in Bangladesh.

Exclusion & Ostracism

Subtle

Deliberately leaving someone out of online groups, conversations, or communities to isolate and humiliate them.

Signs Someone May Be Experiencing Cyberbullying

These signs can appear in yourself or in someone you care about. Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, it probably is.

Becoming upset, anxious, or withdrawn after using their phone or computer

Suddenly avoiding social media or online platforms they used to enjoy

Being secretive or evasive about their online activity

Unexplained changes in mood, sleep, or appetite

Reluctance to attend school or social events

Receiving an unusual number of messages or notifications

Talking about feeling helpless, worthless, or wanting to disappear

If someone talks about wanting to disappear or harm themselves — take it seriously. Reach out to a trusted adult or contact a mental health helpline immediately. You could save a life.

Prevention & Protection

You have more control than you think. These practical steps can significantly reduce your risk online.

01

Protect Your Privacy

Review your privacy settings on all platforms. Limit who can see your posts, contact you, or find your profile. Never share your location, phone number, or home address publicly.

02

Think Before You Share

Once something is online, it can be very difficult to remove. Before posting, ask yourself: would I be comfortable if anyone — including a stranger — could see this?

03

Use Block & Report Tools

Every major platform has tools to block users and report abusive content. Use them without hesitation. Blocking someone is not weakness — it is a boundary.

04

Keep Evidence

If you experience harassment, take screenshots before blocking or deleting. Document dates, times, and platforms. This evidence may be important if you decide to report.

05

Talk to Someone You Trust

You do not have to handle this alone. Tell a parent, teacher, counselor, or trusted friend. Speaking up is one of the most powerful things you can do.
