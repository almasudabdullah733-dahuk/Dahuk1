Understanding Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is the repeated use of digital platforms — social media, messaging apps, or online games — to harm, harass, threaten, or humiliate someone. It includes things like hurtful comments, spreading rumors, sharing private content without consent, and deliberate exclusion.
Cyberbullying usually involves a pattern of repeated behavior, but a single serious incident — such as a threat, non-consensual image sharing, or doxing — can still cause real harm and is always worth reporting, even if it only happens once.
Harassment through comments and messages, impersonation with fake accounts, and non-consensual sharing of photos are among the most reported forms. Visit our Awareness & Education page for a full breakdown.
Yes. While Dahuk focuses on young people aged 13–25, online harassment affects people of all ages. Adults experiencing harassment are welcome to use our reporting form and reach out to the same organizations listed on our Resources page.
The terms overlap heavily. "Cyberbullying" is often used for repeated behavior among peers, especially students, while "online harassment" is a broader term that can include strangers, adults, and one-off but severe incidents. Dahuk treats both seriously.
